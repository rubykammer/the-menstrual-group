Most hurtful comments about periods aren't meant to hurt. They come from awkwardness, or from a joke that's been around so long nobody questions it. Fixing this doesn't take a script. It takes a little thought about what your words are really saying.

## The cheat sheet

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| "Are you on your period or something?" | "You seem upset. What's going on?" |
| "It can't be that bad." | "That sounds rough. What would help?" |
| "You're being really hormonal." | "I can tell this matters to you. Tell me more." |
| "My ex never had this problem." | Nothing. Please say nothing. |
| "Why don't you just take something?" | "Can I grab you anything?" |
| "Ugh, gross." | "No worries, I'll get that." |

## Why "are you on your period?" lands so badly

It takes a real feeling and turns it into a symptom. Even when hormones are playing a part, the thing they're upset about is usually still true. PMS can lower someone's patience. It rarely makes up the problem.

If your partner brings up the same concern in a calm week, you'll wish you had listened the first time.

## Why comparing never helps

You may have a colleague whose periods are no big deal, or a sister who powers through. That has nothing to do with the person in front of you. Pain is hard to compare, and severe period pain can be a sign of a medical condition. Start from believing them.

## When to raise the pattern

If you've noticed that the hard days do follow a pattern, and it's affecting you both, it's okay to bring it up. Timing is everything, so do it when things are calm, and make it about teamwork:

> "I've noticed the week before your period seems really hard for you lately. Is there anything we could do differently during that week?"

That's an invitation. It isn't an accusation, and your partner gets to say no.

## Talking to other men

If you're a man, some of the most useful things you can do happen when no women are around. When a friend makes the tired joke, you don't have to give a lecture. "Come on, that's a bit much" is plenty. Or just ask, "What do you mean by that?" and let it hang there.

> **If you say the wrong thing:** you probably will at some point. Skip the defense and try "That came out badly. I'm sorry. I'm listening." It works better than you'd expect.
