Many people were taught that periods are simply meant to be miserable, so they put up with things a doctor could help with. A rough day now and then is common. A period that takes over your life isn't something you have to accept.

## What usually falls within normal

- A cycle of 21 to 35 days for adults. Teens can be more irregular, because cycles can take years to settle.
- Bleeding for 2 to 7 days.
- Blood that is red, pink or brown, depending on how heavy the flow is.
- Cramps, bloating, tender breasts or low mood that are annoying but manageable.

## Signs that bleeding may be too heavy

- Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row
- Bleeding that lasts more than 7 days
- Needing to double up on protection, or change it during the night
- Clots the size of a quarter or bigger

Heavy bleeding can lead to anemia, so it's worth raising even if it has always been like this for you.

## Other reasons to book an appointment

- Pain that stops you from working, studying or doing your usual things
- Periods that suddenly become more painful, heavier or irregular
- Bleeding between periods, after sex, or after menopause
- Periods that stop for three months or more, when you aren't pregnant
- No first period by age 15

Conditions such as endometriosis, fibroids, thyroid problems and PCOS (which you may now also see called PMOS) can all change how periods behave. Many are very treatable once someone looks.

## When to get help right away

Get urgent medical care if you feel faint or dizzy with heavy bleeding, have sudden severe pelvic pain, or might be pregnant and are bleeding heavily or in pain.

## Getting the most out of the appointment

Keep a simple log for a month or two: dates, how heavy the flow was, and how bad the pain was on a scale of 1 to 10. Writing down what it stops you doing helps too. "I miss a day of work every month" tells a doctor more than "it's pretty bad."

> **If you're a partner:** you can't decide what's normal for someone else, but you can notice a pattern and gently say so. Try "You've been really wiped out by this lately. Want me to come along to an appointment?"
