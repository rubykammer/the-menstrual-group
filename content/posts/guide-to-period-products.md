The period aisle can be overwhelming, whether you're choosing for yourself or standing there with a list on your phone. There's no best product. There's only the one that suits a person's body, flow and day.

## At a glance

| Product | Worn | Change or empty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pads | Inside underwear | At least every 4 to 8 hours |
| Tampons | Inside the vagina | At least every 4 to 8 hours |
| Menstrual cups | Inside the vagina | Every 8 to 12 hours |
| Menstrual discs | Inside the vagina | Follow the instructions on the pack |
| Period underwear | Worn as underwear | Follow the instructions on the pack |

Change sooner if your flow is heavy, or if a product feels full or uncomfortable.

## Pads

Pads are the easiest place to start. They stick to underwear, come in lots of lengths and thicknesses, and nothing goes inside the body. Reusable fabric versions are also available. They can feel bulky, and they aren't ideal for swimming.

## Tampons

Tampons are small and discreet, and you can swim in them. Use the lowest absorbency that your flow needs. A tampon shouldn't be left in for longer than the instructions say, so set a reminder on your phone if you tend to forget.

## Cups and discs

These are flexible cups that collect blood, and many are reusable for years, which can save money and reduce waste. There's a learning curve, so give it a few cycles. Wash your hands first, and follow the cleaning instructions carefully.

## Period underwear

These look like normal underwear, with absorbent layers built in. They're comfortable, and good for overnight or as backup, although a heavy flow may need something extra.

## A word on toxic shock syndrome

Toxic shock syndrome (TSS) is a rare but serious infection that can occur when using a tampon or menstrual cup. It develops quickly. Symptoms can include a sudden high temperature, vomiting, a sunburn-like rash, and feeling dizzy or confused.

If you get these symptoms, remove the tampon or cup and get medical help right away. Say that you were using one.

> **If you're a partner:** when you're sent to buy supplies, note the brand, the type and the absorbency. Take a photo of the box. If you stand in the aisle confused, that's fine. Just call.
