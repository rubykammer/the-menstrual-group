Most of us got one rushed lesson on this at school, if we got one at all. So let's start again, with no diagrams of a cross-section and no embarrassed teachers.

The menstrual cycle is the monthly process that prepares the body for a possible pregnancy. Day one is the first day of a period, and the cycle runs until the day before the next period starts.

## How long is a cycle?

The textbook number is 28 days, but very few bodies read the textbook. Adult cycles commonly run anywhere from 21 to 35 days, and the length can shift from month to month. Bleeding usually lasts between 2 and 7 days.

## The four phases

**Menstruation.** If no pregnancy has happened, the lining of the uterus sheds. This is the period. It usually adds up to somewhere between one and five tablespoons of fluid, even though it can feel like more.

**The follicular phase.** Technically this starts on day one and overlaps with the period. Estrogen rises, the lining rebuilds, and an egg matures in one of the ovaries.

**Ovulation.** An ovary releases the egg, about 12 to 16 days before the next period. The egg survives for only about a day, but sperm can survive for several days. That's why the fertile window is longer than a single day.

**The luteal phase.** Progesterone rises to prepare the lining. If the egg isn't fertilized, hormone levels fall, the lining sheds, and the whole thing starts again.

## Why it affects more than the uterus

Estrogen and progesterone act throughout the body, so a cycle can touch sleep, skin, appetite, digestion, energy and mood. Everyone's pattern is different. Some people barely notice their cycle. Others plan around it.

## Common myths

- **"You can't get pregnant on your period."** It's less likely, but it can happen, especially with shorter cycles.
- **"Everyone ovulates on day 14."** Only if their cycle happens to fit the averages. Ovulation timing varies, even for the same person.
- **"Cycles sync up when people live together."** It's a popular idea, but the evidence for it is weak.

> **If you're a partner:** you don't need to memorize hormone charts. Knowing that a cycle is a whole month of changes, and not five days of bleeding, already puts you ahead of most people.
