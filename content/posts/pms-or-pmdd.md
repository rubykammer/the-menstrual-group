PMS is a punchline far too often. It's also a real set of symptoms, and at the severe end, it's a condition that deserves treatment.

## What PMS looks like

Premenstrual syndrome is a mix of physical and emotional symptoms that shows up a week or two before a period and eases once it starts. More than 90% of women say they get some premenstrual symptoms, including bloating, headaches and moodiness. For most, they're mild.

## What PMDD looks like

Premenstrual dysphoric disorder is a severe form of PMS. It affects less than 5% of women of childbearing age. The timing is the same, but the symptoms are much stronger, and can include:

- Deep sadness, hopelessness or anxiety
- Intense irritability or anger
- Feeling out of control or overwhelmed
- Trouble with work, school or relationships
- Thoughts of suicide

The key difference is impact. PMS is unpleasant. PMDD gets in the way of daily life.

## What helps

**Track it.** Noting symptoms every day for two or three cycles shows whether they follow your cycle. That record is also very useful to a doctor.

**Look after the basics.** Regular exercise, enough sleep, and steady meals help many people with milder symptoms.

**Ask about treatment.** For severe symptoms, a doctor may suggest antidepressants called SSRIs, hormonal options or talking therapy. Help exists, and asking for it isn't an overreaction.

> **Need help right now?** If you or someone you love is having thoughts of suicide, please don't wait. In the US, call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline at any time. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number.

## If it's someone you love

Don't diagnose your partner, and please never say "it's just your PMDD" during a disagreement. Pick a calm moment in a good week, say what you've noticed, and ask how you can help. Later, you can agree together on a plan for hard days: what helps, what doesn't, and who to call.

> **If you're a partner:** two things can be true. Symptoms can be intense, and how you both treat each other still matters. Support doesn't mean accepting cruelty, and needing support doesn't make someone a burden. If it's hard on you too, counseling can help you both.
