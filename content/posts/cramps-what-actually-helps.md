Cramps are caused by natural chemicals called prostaglandins, which are made in the lining of the uterus. They make the uterus contract so it can shed that lining. More prostaglandins usually means stronger cramps, and that explains why pain tends to be worst on the first day or two.

## Heat

A heating pad or hot water bottle on the lower belly is simple and it works for a lot of people. A warm bath or shower helps too. Wrap anything hot in a towel, and never fall asleep on a heating pad.

## Pain relief, taken early

Over-the-counter anti-inflammatory medicines such as ibuprofen and naproxen lower prostaglandin levels. Timing matters: they work best at the first sign of your period or cramps, and not after the pain has built up.

Check the label, and ask a pharmacist first if you have asthma, stomach or kidney problems, are pregnant, or take other medicines.

## Moving, gently

It may be the last thing you feel like doing, but regular aerobic exercise such as walking, swimming or cycling can ease period pain. It doesn't have to be a workout. A short walk or some stretching on the living room floor counts.

## Sleep and massage

Getting enough sleep before and during your period makes a difference. So can massaging your belly and lower back.

## If it's still bad

Hormonal contraception can make periods lighter and less painful, and a TENS machine, which uses mild electrical pulses, may help too. A doctor can talk through what would suit you.

Severe cramps aren't something you need to push through. See a doctor if pain keeps you from your usual activities, gets worse over time, or doesn't respond to pain relief. Pain like that can be a sign of a condition such as [endometriosis](/blog/endometriosis-guide).

> **If you're a partner:** learn where the heating pad lives and keep pain relief stocked. Being the person who shows up with it, without being asked, beats any speech you could make.
