The World Health Organization estimates that endometriosis affects about 10% of women of reproductive age worldwide, or around 190 million people. Even so, many have never heard of it until they've been in pain for years.

## What it is

Endometriosis is a chronic condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside the uterus, causing inflammation and scar tissue. It can start with a person's first period and last through to menopause.

## Common symptoms

- Severe pain during periods
- Pain during or after sex
- Pain when going to the toilet
- Long-term pelvic pain
- Tiredness, nausea or digestive problems
- Difficulty getting pregnant
- Low mood or anxiety

Symptoms differ a lot from one person to the next, and the amount of pain doesn't always match how much of the condition is present.

## Why diagnosis takes so long

Symptoms overlap with many other conditions, and period pain has a long history of being brushed off. Some people are told it's normal for years. Tests can include scans, and sometimes a keyhole operation called a laparoscopy, and a normal scan doesn't always rule it out.

If pain is affecting your life and you feel dismissed, it's reasonable to ask for a second opinion or a referral to a specialist.

## How it's treated

There's currently no cure, but symptoms can be managed. Options include pain relief, hormonal treatments and surgery. The right plan depends on the person's symptoms, side effects and whether they want children, and it may change over time.

## How to support someone with endometriosis

**Believe them.** They may have spent years being told it's nothing. You can be the person who doesn't do that.

**Learn about it.** Reading this article counts. Reading their test results, if they want you to, counts more.

**Go with them.** A second person at appointments can take notes, ask questions and help them feel heard.

**Stay flexible.** Plans may get cancelled, and that isn't personal. Keep inviting them anyway.

**Be patient about intimacy.** If sex hurts, talk about it openly and without pressure. Find the kinds of closeness that feel good for you both.

**Get support for yourself.** Chronic pain affects relationships. It's okay to find this hard too. A counselor or a partners' support group can give you somewhere to take that.

> **If you're a partner:** you can't fix endometriosis, and it won't help to try. Your job is to be steady, informed and on their side, especially on the days nothing helps.
