If you've never had a period, it's easy to feel like this is a part of your partner's life you're meant to stay out of. You don't need to take it over, but you can make it a lot easier to live with. Most of being a good partner here comes down to noticing, asking and doing.

## Start by asking

Everyone wants something different. One person wants to be left alone on the sofa. Another wants company and a foot rub. Ask once, on a calm day: "What actually helps when you're on your period? What makes it worse?" Then remember the answer.

## Do the small things

- Keep a heating pad or hot water bottle where you can find it.
- Keep pain relief and their preferred products in the house, and pick some up without making a big deal of it.
- Take over a chore or two without being asked.
- Cook dinner, or order it.
- Change the plans without complaint if they need a quiet night.

## Buy the products without flinching

If they ask you to pick up tampons, buy the tampons. Take a photo of the box so you get the right one. Nobody at the checkout cares. If you're not sure, call and ask. That beats guessing, and being a person who can do this is quietly very attractive.

## Handle the mess like an adult

A stain on the sheets isn't a crisis. Rinse it in cold water, put it in the wash, and move on. Your calm response tells your partner that their body isn't something to apologize for.

## Be careful with mood talk

Never ask "are you on your period?" in the middle of a disagreement. Even if the timing happens to line up, it tells your partner that their feelings don't count. Listen to the concern itself. There's more on this in [what to say, and what not to](/blog/what-to-say-and-what-not-to).

## Sex is a conversation

Some people are more interested in sex during their period, and some are not at all. Both are fine. Ask, respect the answer, and keep a towel handy if you're both into it.

## Take the long view

Learn roughly when the harder days tend to fall, if your partner is happy to share that. Don't use it as a way to predict or explain their mood. Use it to plan a lighter week.

> **The short version:** ask what helps, do it without being asked twice, and never make your partner feel like a problem to be managed.
