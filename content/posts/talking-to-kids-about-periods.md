The average first period arrives at around age 12, but it can come as early as 8. A child who knows what's coming is far better off than one who finds out in a school bathroom, so don't wait for a perfect moment.

## Make it lots of small talks

It doesn't need to be one big, formal sit-down. Use whatever comes up: a question about the tampons in the bathroom, a scene on TV, a school lesson. Short, relaxed and often beats long and intense.

A good opener is simply asking "What do you already know about periods?" Then go from there.

## Use real words

Say "vagina", "uterus" and "period". Using the proper words shows there's nothing shameful here, and it gives kids the language to ask for help if something is wrong.

## Boys need this too

Your son will grow up to have classmates, friends, colleagues and maybe a partner who have periods. Boys who understand periods are far less likely to tease, and far more likely to be kind. Teach them the same facts, at the same age.

## Dads, this is your job too

If you're a dad raising a daughter, particularly on your own, you might feel out of your depth. You don't need to be an expert. Learn the basics, stay calm, and make it clear she can come to you. These are the only qualifications.

- Keep pads in the bathroom before they're needed, and show her where they are.
- Ask if she'd like to talk to an aunt, a family friend or a doctor as well. Having another trusted adult is a bonus, and it doesn't mean you've failed.
- Shop for supplies together, or just restock without comment.

## Put a small kit together

A little pouch for her school bag takes the fear out of a first period happening away from home. Include a couple of pads, spare underwear, and a plastic bag for anything that needs to go home.

## What to tell them to expect

- The first periods may be irregular, and cycles can take a few years to settle.
- A period usually lasts between 2 and 7 days.
- Pads should be changed at least every 4 to 8 hours.
- It looks like more blood than it really is.

See a doctor if periods haven't started by 15, if they're very heavy, or if pain is making her miss school.

> **The goal:** you're not aiming for a flawless talk. You're aiming to be the person they feel okay coming back to.
